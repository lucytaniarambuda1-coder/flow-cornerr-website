Prompting 101

The difference between a mediocre AI answer and a genuinely useful one usually comes down to how you ask.

← Back to Understand & Use AI

By Lucy Mukhethwa · AI Projects Lead & Career Strategist

Most people's first experience with AI chat tools is underwhelming — a vague question gets a vague, generic answer. The good news: getting dramatically better results isn't about learning to "code" or master some secret technique. It's about being a clearer communicator. Here's how.

1. Be Specific

"Write me a CV bullet point" will get you something generic. "Rewrite this bullet point to lead with a measurable result, in one sentence, for a customer service role" will get you something usable. The more specific your request, the less generic the output.

2. Give Context

Tell the tool who you are, what you're trying to achieve, and who the output is for. For example: "I'm applying for a graduate role in banking. Rewrite this paragraph to sound confident but not arrogant, for a recruiter reading dozens of applications."

3. Iterate — Don't Expect Perfection First Try

Treat the first response as a draft, not a final answer. Follow up with "make it shorter," "make it sound more like me," or "give me three alternative versions." Refining in conversation almost always beats trying to write the perfect prompt upfront.

4. Break Big Tasks Into Steps

Instead of "help me with my job search," try smaller, specific asks: "help me identify the top 5 keywords in this job ad," then "check if my CV includes those keywords," then "suggest where I could naturally work in the ones that are missing."

5. Always Fact-Check

Never take a specific fact, statistic, or claim from an AI response at face value if it matters. Verify it yourself before you rely on it or repeat it to someone else.

Try It Yourself

Next time you're stuck on a CV bullet or a LinkedIn line, try this prompt: "Rewrite this to lead with a specific, quantified result, in one sentence, for a [your target role] application: [paste your text]." Notice how much more useful the result is than just asking it to "make this better."
