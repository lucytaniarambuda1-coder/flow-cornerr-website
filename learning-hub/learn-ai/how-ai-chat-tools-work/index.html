How Tools Like ChatGPT & Claude Actually Work

A plain-language look under the hood — so you know what you're really working with.

← Back to Understand & Use AI

By Lucy Mukhethwa · AI Projects Lead & Career Strategist

Tools like ChatGPT and Claude can feel like magic — you type a question, and a thoughtful, well-written answer appears in seconds. Here's what's actually happening, in plain language.

They Predict, They Don't "Know"

These tools are built on large language models — systems trained on enormous amounts of text to learn the patterns of language: which words and ideas tend to follow which others. When you ask a question, the model isn't looking up a stored answer. It's generating a response one piece at a time, predicting what should come next based on everything it learned during training.

That's a subtle but important distinction. It means these tools are exceptional at producing fluent, well-structured, plausible-sounding text — but "plausible-sounding" and "correct" aren't always the same thing.

What They're Genuinely Good At

  • Drafting and rewriting — turning a rough idea into a structured first draft
  • Summarising long documents or explaining a complex topic simply
  • Brainstorming — generating options quickly when you're stuck
  • Editing — tightening wordy sentences, checking tone and clarity

Where They Fall Short

These tools can sometimes state incorrect information with total confidence — often called "hallucinating." They don't automatically know about very recent events unless the tool is specifically connected to the internet. And they don't know you — your actual achievements, your voice, your context — unless you give it to them clearly.

The Practical Takeaway

Use these tools as a fast, capable first-draft partner — never as the final word. Always read critically, fact-check anything that matters, and make sure the final result still sounds like you. That's the difference between using AI well and letting it use you.
